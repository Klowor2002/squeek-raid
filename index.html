🏭 The Factory Floor

Sell surplus at the market (prices per unit): Every real settlement also delivers a sack of sunflower seeds to your silo.

👷 Staff — one hamster, one machine

Staff are not global multipliers: each hired hamster is assigned to a single machine (use the staff menu on the machine card) and only that machine gets the buff. Hiring costs Cogs, once, forever.

🔧 Workshop Improvements

Bought with Cogs. Some are stat gains; the good ones change what the factory can do — more floor, bigger batteries, gentler idle draw, fatter buffers.

📦 Settlement Orders

Every real on-chain settlement spawns a delivery order, sized by that round's settled work. Load the crates before the deadline (counted in settlements) or the customer walks and reputation suffers.

💡 Concept & Devlog — why this is not a dashboard

The chain data is the engine, not the wallpaper. In most "blockchain games" the chain is a scoreboard. Here it is the power grid: the watts your factory can draw this minute are computed from the real wheel's measured speed, and the energy in your batteries only ever grows by the distance you personally witnessed the wheel turn. Unplug the API and the factory starves — that dependency is the game.

The decisions are physical. Machines starve when their inputs run dry and jam when their outputs fill. Power is allocated by priority and by hard circuit switches (ON / IDLE / OFF) — when the wheel slows, you choose which line lives and which line sleeps. Orders arrive on the chain's own cadence, not a scripted timer: a fat settlement means a fat order, due in three settlements whether you are ready or not. Surplus can be sold, crates can rot in the delivery log's memory, and heat from a sprinting hamster must be vented with battery charge you might rather spend on the ovens.

Devlog. v1–v2 of this project were a boss-raid clicker ("SQUEEK RAID"). The owner played it and said, in full: "looks low effort, change the concept entirely." He was right — HP-sponge bosses and a timing bar are a reskin away from any clicker. So the raid was scrapped, not rebalanced: no bosses, no attacks, no timing bar. What survived is only the honest plumbing — the live API reads, the no-retro-credit baseline rule, the RUNNING derivation, and the proof panel — because that plumbing was the part that was actually about Squeekchain. The full red-team critique of this new concept (it is not flattering) is in the repo as CRITIQUE-V3.md.
